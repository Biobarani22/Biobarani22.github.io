01 — About

I'm the person a lab calls when the data is bigger than the hypothesis.

I'm the sole bioinformatics scientist inside a 16–18 member oncology laboratory at Yale School of Medicine, directing genomic analysis for a Phase II breast cancer trial spanning roughly 80 patients across 15–20 US medical centers, a small cell lung cancer trial, and concurrent NSCLC and colorectal programs — alongside an industry collaboration with Genentech.

Across fifteen years I've analyzed 500+ tumor samples and established first-in-lab pipelines for WGS, WES, RNA-seq, ATAC-seq, single-cell, and spatial data. The pattern repeats: arrive where a capability doesn't exist yet, build it so investigators can use it themselves, and replace external-collaborator dependency with reproducible workflows.

What I care about most is the part after the pipeline runs. I'm known for reframing hypotheses on the evidence — including overturning a colorectal program's founding assumption when the data pointed the other way — and for translating high-dimensional results into decision-ready findings for clinicians, pathologists, and bench scientists.

Published in Nature Communications, Molecular Cancer, Cell Reports, Blood Advances, and JITC, with presentations at AACR and SITC. I've peer reviewed 100+ manuscripts for 40+ journals and hold editorial roles at Frontiers in Bioinformatics.

    Yale School of MedicineNew Haven, CT

    Associate Research Scientist, Pathology

    Sole bioinformatics scientist within a 16–18 member oncology laboratory, directing genomic analysis for a Phase II breast cancer trial (~80 patients, 15–20 US medical centers), a small cell lung cancer trial, and concurrent NSCLC and colorectal programs. Supports institution-wide investigators and an industry collaboration with Genentech. Output spans 5 peer-reviewed publications and presentations at AACR and SITC since 2022.

    Clinical trial genomics & biomarker discovery

    • Established a candidate marker of immunotherapy resistance — now advancing to cell line and organoid validation — by profiling transposable element expression across responder and non-responder cohorts within a Phase II breast cancer trial.
    • Delivered the variant evidence underpinning treatment-arm stratification across a multi-center Phase II trial through paired WGS, WES, and RNA-seq analysis, separating response from resistance signatures.
    • Introduced predictive modeling into clinical trial analysis: random forest classifiers for treatment response, plus agentic AI workflows for interrogating multi-dimensional genomic and clinical datasets.

    Multi-omics discovery & mechanistic interpretation

    • Surfaced elevated PCDH11X expression in NSCLC brain metastases — published in Molecular Cancer (2026) and the anchor of a successful Department of Defense PRCRP Idea Award application.
    • Produced the first patient-level evidence that lung cancer brain metastases evade immune detection via reduced HLA class I antigen presentation rather than defective interferon-γ signaling, integrating WES, RNA-seq, and ATAC-seq.
    • Identified TAP2 downregulation as a driver of immune evasion and immunotherapy resistance in NSCLC, pairing genome-wide expression profiling with ATAC-seq assessment of chromatin accessibility across TAP2 regulatory states.
    • Overturned a colorectal program's hypothesis by showing that high rather than low MGMT expression associates with worse disease — redirecting study design and prompting cell line validation (JITC, 2025).

    Pipeline development, automation & team enablement

    • Accelerated genomic analysis turnaround by 60%+ via the laboratory's first in-house WES, WGS, and ATAC-seq pipelines, architected to replace external-collaborator dependency with reproducible, investigator-accessible workflows.
    • Eliminated a multi-day manual bottleneck in quantitative immunofluorescence analysis by scripting the workflow in Python and R, collapsing spreadsheet-based marker quantification into a single automated run for pathologists and immunologists.
    • Built the lab's computational capability — training bench scientists in analytical methods, mentoring junior researchers toward independent careers, and directing sequencing and analysis strategy with investigators in weekly review.
